# Disease Interactions

MedSynth models several clinically significant disease interactions while strictly avoiding the duplication of clinical logic. The interactions are orchestrated using the dynamic attribute and symptom architectures.

## Supported Interactions

### HIV and Tuberculosis
- **Evidence:** Strong WHO and Nigerian evidence demonstrates that HIV is the strongest risk factor for developing active TB.
- **Implementation:** The `hiv.json` module sets the `hiv_positive` attribute to `true`. The `tuberculosis.json` module actively checks `hiv_positive == true` during its annual evaluation loop, significantly increasing the transition probability into active TB.
- **Status:** Fully functional.

### Sickle Cell Disease and Malaria/Meningitis/LRI
- **Evidence:** Strong clinical evidence shows that febrile illnesses (particularly Malaria in Nigeria) frequently trigger acute vaso-occlusive crises (VOC) in patients with Sickle Cell Disease (SCD).
- **Implementation:** The `sickle_cell_disease.json` module evaluates the patient's symptoms during its monthly loop. If it detects `Fever > 0` (which is naturally generated by the `malaria.json`, `meningitis.json`, or `lower_respiratory_infections.json` modules), it bypasses the standard baseline crisis risk and triggers an acute VOC crisis with high probability.
- **Status:** Fully functional.

### Age and Ischemic Heart Disease / Stroke
- **Evidence:** Cardiovascular risk scales exponentially with age.
- **Implementation:** Both `ischemic_heart_disease.json` and `stroke.json` use multi-tier age-gating `ComplexTransitions` to ensure prevalence aligns with older populations.
- **Status:** Fully functional.

## Unsupported Interactions (Limitations)

### Sickle Cell Disease and Stroke
- **Evidence:** SCD children (HbSS) have a massively elevated risk of ischemic stroke.
- **Limitation:** The GMF architecture does not allow a module to forcefully trigger an explicit target state in *another* module without `CallSubmodule` (which is not implemented here). To avoid duplicating the entire Stroke clinical pathway inside the SCD module, this direct link was omitted. SCD patients remain eligible for standard strokes via age, but the disease-specific spike is unmodelled.

### Severe Malaria and Anemia (Transfusions)
- **Evidence:** Severe malaria frequently causes profound anemia requiring blood transfusion.
- **Limitation:** While Malaria accurately models the encounter and treatment, the underlying mechanism does not currently track a numeric `hemoglobin` continuous variable. Transfusions are represented as a fixed `Procedure` directly in the SCD module, but Malaria treats severity via mortality probabilities rather than dynamically calculating anemia thresholds.
